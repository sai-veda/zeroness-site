Privacy Policy

Effective November 8, 2026

Zeroness is an email app that works with Gmail and Google Workspace accounts, made by Applied Crew, Inc. ("we"). This policy explains what data Zeroness accesses, how it is used, where it is stored, who it is shared with and how to delete it.

Google data Zeroness accesses

When you connect a Google account, you choose to grant Zeroness these permissions through Google's consent screen:

  • Gmail (gmail.modify): read your mail; organize it (label, archive, star, mark read or unread, move to trash); and send messages and drafts in your mailbox.
  • Basic profile (openid, email, profile): your email address and name, to identify your account.
  • Google Calendar events (calendar.events): read your calendar and, only when you ask, create, change or answer events.
  • Google Calendar, read-only (calendar.readonly), requested by the iPhone app: read your calendars to show your schedule next to your mail. It cannot change anything.
  • Basic Gmail settings (gmail.settings.basic), requested by the iPhone app: read the addresses you can send from, and, only when you block a sender, create a Gmail filter that moves their future mail to trash. Zeroness does not read or change your other settings, filters, forwarding or vacation replies.

Some permissions are requested only when you first use the feature that needs them, so you may not see all of them on your first sign-in.

How we use it

Only to provide the features you see in Zeroness: showing and searching your mail, sorting it into splits, sign-in code detection, reminders and snoozes, summaries, answering questions you ask about your mail, drafting replies that you review and send, blocking senders you choose to block, and showing your calendar and meeting invites next to your mail. Zeroness never sends mail, changes your calendar or creates a filter without your action, and never permanently deletes mail.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Zeroness's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically:

  • We use Google user data only to provide and improve user-facing features of Zeroness.
  • We do not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to determine credit-worthiness or for lending.
  • We do not use Google user data to develop, improve or train generalized or non-personalized AI or machine-learning models.
  • We transfer Google user data to others only as needed to provide these features (the AI provider and hosting provider below, and Apple for iPhone notifications you turn on), to comply with law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with notice to you.
  • No person at Zeroness reads your email unless you ask us to (for example, for support), it is needed for security or abuse investigations, or the law requires it.

Where your data is stored

For both the web app and the iPhone app, a copy of your mail and your OAuth tokens are stored in Zeroness's database on our hosting provider (Amazon Web Services, United States) so the inbox loads instantly and syncs in the background. The iPhone app also keeps a cached copy of recent mail on your device so it opens instantly and works offline; Google tokens are never stored on the device. Data is encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest, and Google tokens are additionally encrypted with keys managed by AWS Key Management Service (KMS).

Notifications

If you allow notifications in the iPhone app, Zeroness sends the sender's name and the subject of new mail that needs your attention through Apple Push Notification service, which delivers them to your device. The message body is never included. Turn notifications off at any time in iOS Settings.

AI processing

For AI features, the relevant parts of a thread (and, when you send without an attachment, the text you just wrote, to check whether you meant to attach something) are sent to OpenAI's API to produce a summary, classification, check or draft, and the result is returned to Zeroness. OpenAI processes this data under API terms that prohibit using it to train their models; it may retain inputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring and then deletes them. You can turn auto drafts off in Settings.

Read tracking in mail you send

When Tell me when my emails are opened is on (it is by default), each message you send from Zeroness carries one tiny image from zeroness.io. It is an ordinary 1×1 image with a random code in its address; the address contains no name, email address or account id, and your links are never changed. You can turn it off at any time in Settings; that one switch controls it for all mail you send. It stays off automatically for mailing lists and for messages to more than 20 people.

When a recipient's mail app loads that image, we record the time and the kind of client that fetched it (for example Gmail's image proxy, Outlook, Yahoo, Apple Mail or another mail app). To tell those apart we look at the request's user agent and IP address at that moment; neither is stored in our database. Like any web request, it also passes through our hosting provider's standard request logs, which briefly hold the requesting IP address. When you send, we also look up each recipient domain's public mail server (MX) to know which provider hosts it. We use these to show you Opened, Delivered or Bounced per recipient. Delivery failure notices that arrive in your Gmail are matched to the message they are about. Zeroness never loads the image when it shows a message, so when a recipient who also uses Zeroness opens your message there, Zeroness records that open itself, with the time and that recipient's address (only if they were one of the message's recipients), instead of the image. Opening your sent mail in Zeroness is never counted, but an open can't always be told apart from someone else's: reading your own Sent copy in Gmail, or a recipient forwarding the message, can show as an open. After each person's first open, later opens are shown as opened again without naming anyone.

Recipients: an open tells the sender only roughly when the message was opened and with what kind of mail app; if you read it in Zeroness, it tells the sender that you opened it and when. We don't store your IP address or user agent in our database (our hosting provider's request logs briefly hold the IP address, as for any web request), don't set cookies on you, don't build a profile of you, and never share or sell this data. Blocking remote images in your mail app prevents it. Tracking records are deleted 90 days after the message was sent; after that the image still loads but nothing is recorded. To ask about or delete records about you, email sai@appliedcrew.com.

Zeroness for Gmail (Chrome extension)

The optional Zeroness for Gmail extension runs only on mail.google.com, where it shows a welcome offering to open Zeroness, and adds a keyboard shortcut that opens Zeroness in a new tab. It stores a single setting (whether to ask, open Zeroness or stay in Gmail) in Chrome's sync storage on your devices. It does not read your email or the pages you visit, collects no personal data, and sends nothing to Zeroness or anyone else. Removing the extension removes the setting.

What else we collect

Your account email, subscription status and crash reports. If you leave Share anonymous usage analytics on (Settings), the web app also records which features you use, such as opening a thread, sending a reply, snoozing or pressing a keyboard shortcut, and makes session recordings that show how the app is used: clicks, scrolling and page changes, with all text, form fields and images hidden, so a recording never shows what is in your mail. These records are tied to a random account id, never your email address, and never include the content of your mail, who it is from or to, or what you search for or ask. They are processed by PostHog (United States), our analytics provider, under a data processing agreement, and recordings are deleted after 30 days. Turn the setting off at any time; your browser's Do Not Track setting is respected. No advertising trackers. Remote images in mail load through Zeroness so senders don't see your IP address, and known tracking pixels are removed.

Retention and deletion

We keep your data while your account is connected. When you remove an account in Zeroness, its cached mail, AI outputs and tokens are deleted from our database within 7 days. You can also revoke Zeroness's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or email sai@appliedcrew.com to delete your account and everything we hold; we complete requests within 7 days. Encrypted backups expire within a further 7 days, so deleted data is gone from backups within 14 days at most.

Children

Zeroness is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data.
